# Cascade experiment

Would checking an answer and escalating to the next tier beat always using the medium tier? Best cascade: **M->L**, +5.0 points of pass rate at 7.1x always-M's cost: **does not clear the bar** (+2 points at no more than 2x the cost).

Generated by `py -m gearbox.eval.cascade report` from stored answers, judge scores and one batch of checks (`data/v2/checks.jsonl`). The escalation threshold was chosen on the training split; the table below is the test split, scored once.

## How well the check spots failing answers

| answers checked | min_score (from train) | precision | recall | F1 | escalation rate | failing share |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| S | 4 | 0.94 | 0.96 | 0.95 | 69% | 67% |
| M | 4 | 0.84 | 0.81 | 0.82 | 21% | 22% |

Precision: of the answers it escalated, the share that really failed. Recall: of the answers that failed, the share it caught.

Checks: 1200 answers (166 decided by free signals, 5 checks unreadable); checker spend GBP 0.096 (batch). Cascade costs include the check at list price and, when it escalates, both answers.

## Where M->L escalates (test split)

25 escalations, 14 of them decided by a free signal (an empty answer, a cut-off one, or Haiku saying it can't verify) with no checker call. By source: simpleqa 16, aime2025 5, mmlu-pro 2, dolly 1, triviaqa 1. These are the prompt types where the probe found Sonnet worth its price (niche facts, competition maths).
